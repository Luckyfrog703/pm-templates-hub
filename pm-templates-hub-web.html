Case Study · PM Templates Hub

My certificate, made tangible.

One-liner: A working library of 30 project management templates — the Google PM Certificate's document set, made fillable, saveable, and reusable.

The problem

New PMs learn the documents — charter, RACI, risk register, status / executive / impact reports — as course exercises, then start their first job and rebuild them from memory or hunt random templates online. The knowledge exists; a working toolkit doesn't.

What I found

  • The documents cluster by lifecycle phase (initiation, planning, execution, closing) — and that's how practitioners actually reach for them.
  • A template alone is half the value. The other half is when to use it, who reads it, and what good looks like — which is what courses teach and template sites omit.

Decisions

  • 30 templates organized by phase, with Executive Report and Impact Report featured — the two executives actually read.
  • Every template carries when-to-use guidance, audience, section-by-section instructions, worked example phrasing, and a pre-share checklist.
  • “Use this template” creates a fillable, saveable document with progress %, draft / final status, duplicate, and copy-as-text — a toolkit, not a PDF graveyard.
  • This public version is a static rebuild for GitHub Pages: the same library and workspace, with documents saved in the visitor's own browser instead of on a server.

Metrics I'd watch with real users

Templates started vs. finished (% completed), repeat use per user, and which templates get favorited by phase — what do new PMs actually need?

Roadmap

v2: a “starter pack” flow — pick a project type, get the 6 documents you need in order. v3: export to Docs / Word formats.

60-second talk track

“This one is my certificate made tangible. The Google PM course teaches about thirty documents; I built them into a working hub where each template explains when to use it and what good looks like, and you can fill in and save your own versions. An interviewer doesn't have to take the certificate on faith — I can open this and talk through when I'd issue an impact report versus a status report, and why they go to different audiences.”

Tough questions

“Aren't templates commodity?” → “Blank templates are. Guidance plus a saveable workflow isn't — the product is the judgment layer around the documents.”

“Which template matters most?” → “The risk register, because it's the only one that's about the future. Everything else reports the present or past.”
